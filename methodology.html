Research integrity

How we measure Schenectady.

Methods are public so residents, journalists, researchers, neighborhood groups, and officials can understand what the data can—and cannot—support.

Anonymous by default

No name is required for the research survey. Optional contact information is maintained separately.

Nonpartisan

The initiative does not endorse candidates and does not ask respondents whom they support.

Aggregate reporting

Neighborhood percentages are withheld until the minimum public sample threshold is reached.

Organic sample

Recruitment may include QR posters, events, neighborhood organizations, businesses, libraries, social media, and community partners.

Independent validation

External panels such as Pollfish may later be used as a separately labeled comparison sample rather than silently merged with organic responses.

Known limitations

Voluntary online surveys can overrepresent highly engaged residents. Self-reported voting is not a replacement for official election records.

Scoring policy

No invented scores.

The Civic Power Index and Melrah Community Sustainability Score will remain unpublished until indicators, weights, official data sources, geographic coverage, sample sizes, and validation rules are documented.

Initial threshold

Minimum n = 25

This initial threshold is a privacy and interpretation safeguard. It is not a claim that 25 responses make a neighborhood sample statistically representative, and it may be raised as the study matures.
